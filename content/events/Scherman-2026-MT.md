Title: Small Interactions, Big Possibilities: A Journey from Molecular Recognition to Dynamic Materials
EventType: Talk
Speaker: Oren A. Scherman
Status: published
Date: 2026-11-13
Time: 18:30-19:30
Location: Winstanley Lecture Theatre
EventID: Scherman-2026-MT

How can interactions between individual molecules determine whether a material flows, stretches, heals or releases a drug? And how can understanding those interactions lead to technologies that were never part of the original plan?

This talk follows our exploration of cucurbituril, a molecular host capable of bringing guest molecules together in water. Beginning with the fundamentals of molecular recognition, I will describe how we translated a binding motif into reversible connections between polymer chains, creating hydrogels whose behaviour depends on interactions that continually break and reform. From these foundations, the research developed along two complementary paths: biomaterials for local drug delivery, including approaches to glioblastoma, and high-performance materials with remarkable toughness, compressibility and biomimetic behaviour. Bringing these paths together now offers opportunities to tune material mechanics and develop responsive delivery systems for applications such as osteoarthritis.

Alongside these developments, we have continued to ask fundamental questions about the interactions themselves, developing optical approaches to examine supramolecular complexes at the single-molecule level. I will also describe an unexpected diversion into electrolytes for redox flow batteries and the formation of a spin-out company. Throughout, the story highlights the role of collaboration, the value of following interesting results, and the sometimes unpredictable relationship between fundamental chemistry and useful technologies.
