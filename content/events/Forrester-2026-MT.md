Title: The nose knows - What thermal imaging tells us about your stress state
EventType: Talk
Speaker: Gillian Forrester
Status: published
Date: 2026-10-23
Time: 18:30
Location: Winstanley Lecture Theatre
EventID: Forrester-2026-MT

We can often tell how someone is feeling by looking at their facial expressions, but emotion is more than skin deep. Advancements in technology are allowing scientists to visualise human and other great ape stress responses using thermal imaging. Professor Gilly Forrester will share her lab’s exciting new research detailing how the way blood flows in our faces reflects the stress state of our bodies. A live thermal imaging demonstration will show changing stress states in real time based on nasal temperature. Gilly’s research aims to increase our understanding of the relationships between stress, resilience, self-regulation, and psychological health. One of her aims is to use this approach to improve wellbeing of apes in captivity and to increase success rates of great ape rewilding efforts.

**About the speaker:** <br>
Gilly is a Professor of Comparative Cognition in the School of Psychology at the University of Sussex. She researches with great apes and babies, studying how our brains and behaviours evolved and develop over time. She earned a BSc in Cognitive Science from the University of California, San Diego and a PhD in Experimental Neuroscience from the University of Oxford. By studying both human and non-human primates, Gilly is on a quest to better understand how we became the upright-walking, talking, tool-using great apes that we are today. She is dedicated to science engagement. Gilly founded the Me, Human project, directs Science Futures at Glastonbury Festival and makes regular contributions to television, radio, newsprint, and podcasts. Gilly has recently worked with Jim Al-Khalili on his new science YouTube cannel Unbaffled Science and she is also the current guest on BBC Radio4’s The Life Scientific (airing late September 2026).
